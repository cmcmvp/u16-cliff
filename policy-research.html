03 — Preliminary policy research

Plenty of policy.
Little of it about hockey.

A literature review of government support, institutions, infrastructure and the private sector

Introduction Government & policies Institutions International exchange Infrastructure Private sector Assessment

Introduction

The development of ice sports in China has received huge support from the government, with many policies set to facilitate the sport across the nation. Whereas some aspects are improving, other challenges limit their development and popularity. This review examines the factors behind the drop in youth hockey participation around age sixteen and the sport's relatively limited reach, even as China's player base has grown considerably in recent years.

Government & policies

Supportive policies were promulgated in order to popularise ice sports. For example, there was the plan to engage "Healthy China 2030" with participation in this activity reaching 30 million by the year 2030, in order to improve citizens' physical fitness and build their interest in winter sports. However, most participants are skiing or free skating, so the ice hockey community did not benefit much from this activity.

The Development Plan for Ice and Snow Sports (2016–2025) set particular targets for enhancing participation, clearly reflecting that the government attaches more importance to making ice sports a mainstay of Chinese culture.

Government & institutions

The government has united with institutions of sports education and with companies in training specialised personnel — both athletes and coaches. Training camps and schools were opened to help young people develop their skills, laying a foundation for the long-term development of ice sports.

Government & international exchanges

The government has supported the arrangement of activities and international exchanges. Hosting the Beijing Winter Olympics further increased China's influence in winter sports and encouraged many more people to participate. China has also brought in more international competitions, enabling local athletes to compete with the best and thereby increasing interest in winter sports.

Infrastructure

Infrastructural development has been one of the major priorities. The Beijing Winter Olympics brought great changes to ice venues and their surroundings — most importantly within Beijing itself. Local governments and businesses should be further encouraged to invest in ice rinks that would make ice hockey accessible to all.

Private sector

The private sector has played a very important role. Businesses have invested in the ice hockey industry through sponsorships and partnerships, which in turn support related industries such as tourism and equipment manufacturing. This involvement stimulates a positive growth spiral and makes ice sports more appealing to the general public.

Overall assessment

There are indeed activities and government spending on ice sports promotion. However, these policies are neither specifically directed at ice hockey, nor do they have a substantial effect in structurally changing the ice hockey community and industry.

Where reform is needed

Three structural levers

🎓 Educational system

Creating a viable dual-career pathway so that being a student and an athlete are not mutually exclusive after sixteen.

📣 Public awareness

Shifting perception away from "dangerous, expensive and obscure" toward a normal part of youth culture.

💼 Commercialised industry

Building a business ecosystem that gives players a future in the sport beyond their playing years.

This research argues that the ice hockey community should be boosted structurally — through reform in the educational system, public awareness, and commercialised industries — rather than through facility construction alone. The next page examines why.

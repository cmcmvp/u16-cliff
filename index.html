01 — Introduction

A fiftyfold rise,
then thin ice at sixteen

China's sports market is growing unboundedly in the twenty-first century. Several large-scale public campaigns were launched in conjunction with the Beijing 2022 Winter Olympics to popularise winter sports across the country, such as the government's "300 Million People on Ice and Snow" initiative. Ice hockey's national participation rose from 1,000 players before 2015 to 58,000 in 2021 — a more than fiftyfold increase.

However, even with improving equipment, better facilities and top coaches arriving from the NHL and KHL, the number of China's youth ice hockey amateurs above the age of sixteen is fewer than 2,000.

Summary

What this project is

Ice hockey plays an important role in promoting physical fitness and skill development, especially among youth. In China, the development of ice hockey has been led by the government — the Beijing Winter Olympics increased public interest and international recognition, alongside large-scale infrastructure investment and private sector participation, indicating a promising future for the industry. Despite these advances, challenges such as an inflexible educational system, poor public awareness and cultural stereotypes make wider take-up difficult to achieve.

This website compares the environments for ice hockey and broader sports in China, the United States and Canada. It focuses on the drop in participation around age sixteen, when many young players leave the sport. Through interviews and a comparison of education and hockey pathways, the analysis explores why staying on the ice becomes so difficult.

Central thesis

The core contradictions within the Chinese sports ecosystem can be understood through a multi-layered theoretical framework: the surface-level community, the deep-level institutional environment, and the root-level cultural differences.

Surface level

Sports socialisation and blending into daily life

Whether hockey is woven into school, friendship and community — or confined to a "small circle".

Deep level

Institutional environments and disciplinary regimes

How governments and sports agencies cultivate talent: facilitative coaching versus closed-door mechanical discipline.

Root level

Structural and cultural foundations

Historical pathways, educational paradigms and the incentives that make academic examination the single certified route to mobility.

Methodology

Global interviews, questionnaires and interactive chat sessions across the United States, Canada and China. The data and information collected is then synthesised and processed into conclusions and further proposals for educational system reform.
